# Scaleout and Vizgard: buyer-situation positioning comparison

**Compared:** 27 September 2026  
**Method:** [Buyer-situation comparison prompt](BUYER_SITUATION_COMPARISON_PROMPT.md), informed by the separate [Scaleout analysis](SCALEOUT_POSITIONING_ANALYSIS.md), [Scaleout assessment](SCALEOUT_POSITIONING_QUALITY_ASSESSMENT.md), [Vizgard analysis](VIZGARD_POSITIONING_ANALYSIS.md) and [Vizgard assessment](VIZGARD_POSITIONING_QUALITY_ASSESSMENT.md). This compares **publicly communicated offers**, not verified product performance or actual buyer preference.

## Scope and source coverage

Rechecked Scaleout's [homepage](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/), [platform](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/platform), [Tactical AI Network](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/solutions/tactical-ai-network), [tactical vision](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/applications/tactical-computer-vision) and [drone AI](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/applications/ai-for-drones), and Vizgard's [homepage](https://vizgard.com/), [FortifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/fortifai/), [UnifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/unifai/) and [VizEdge](https://vizgard.com/product/vizedge/). Private technical briefs, contracts, acceptance reports, buyer interviews and comparative trials were not available. Both sites' maturity and deployment statements are treated as their own claims.

The two decisions below are **illustrative analyst assumptions**, not known procurement requirements. They separate places where the offers could be shortlisted together from places where they serve different stack roles. The buyer collective could include a mission sponsor, operator, technical evaluator, OEM/integrator and programme owner; the actual decision owner is unknown.

## Decision 1: Obtain a visual mission function on deployed equipment

**Illustrative requirement:** A defence or security organisation wants visual detection and operator-relevant outputs from cameras or uncrewed equipment, under limited connectivity, integrated with existing systems. It may also need to evaluate and update models as conditions change. Here the overlapping offers are **Scaleout tactical computer vision, potentially with its Tactical AI Network**, and **Vizgard FortifAI, potentially with UnifAI or VizEdge**. They are plausible alternatives for parts of a visual requirement, but the included function and delivery boundary must be specified before treating them as interchangeable.

[Vizgard FortifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/fortifai/), [Scaleout tactical vision](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/applications/tactical-computer-vision).

## Decision 2: Establish distributed AI/model operations

**Illustrative requirement:** A programme or integrator already has AI models, devices and sites. It needs controlled deployment, visibility, evaluation and updates while data remains under local control and connections vary. Here the closest public comparison is **Scaleout Edge** and **Vizgard UnifAI**. FortifAI or Scaleout tactical vision could be applications on top, but should not be silently included in a platform-only evaluation.

| Same buyer question | Scaleout public position | Vizgard public position | Buyer implication and unknown |
|---|---|---|---|

[Platform](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/platform). | UnifAI is described as an MLOps platform for dataset management, device monitoring, model deployment and operational oversight across distributed AI systems. [UnifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/unifai/). | There is genuine stack overlap. The buyer needs a requirements matrix for model types, workflows, scale, monitoring, APIs, governance and users before concluding functional equivalence. |

Devices can operate disconnected and sync when links return. Vizgard's precise data boundary, cross-site training mechanism and disconnected workflow should be validated; absence of detail is not proof of absence of capability. |

**Positioning reading for this decision:** Scaleout currently makes the stronger *public explanation* of local model improvement across separate sites and how it fits the customer's existing environment. Vizgard communicates a broader operational command-and-control style for AI fleets, but the public UnifAI page leaves availability and architecture questions open. This is about **communicated specificity**, not measured platform capability. [Scaleout platform](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/platform), [Vizgard UnifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/unifai/).

- **FortifAI:** Vizgard's TRL 9 and deployment statements are company claims, supplemented by named testimonials. They apply to FortifAI, not automatically to UnifAI or VizEdge. [Homepage](https://vizgard.com/), [FortifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/fortifai/).
- **Scaleout tactical vision and Scaleout Edge:** The website offers detailed workflows and company-reported demonstrations, plus a defined evaluation path. It does not publish a single public maturity designation or acceptance report that makes a direct FortifAI comparison possible. [Tactical vision](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/applications/tactical-computer-vision), [platform](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/platform), [ISR account](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/post/resilient-edge-ai-for-isr-inside-our-swedish-air-force-demonstration).
- **Drone modules and hardware:** Scaleout says its drone module completed internal integration testing and is ready for structured field evaluation. Vizgard offers VizEdge hardware and claims FortifAI use on uncrewed systems. These statements describe different products and evidence types. [Scaleout drone AI](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/applications/ai-for-drones), [VizEdge](https://vizgard.com/product/vizedge/), [FortifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/fortifai/).

## Overall positioning synthesis

The companies overlap in **edge visual AI and management of distributed models**. They are most likely to enter the same shortlist when a buyer needs a visual capability that must operate on deployed equipment and evolve after deployment. The relative fit changes with the buying question: Vizgard leads its public story with a visual autonomy product and optional hardware; Scaleout leads with distributed AI infrastructure and a local improvement loop, then shows tactical vision and drone workflows built on it. A system integrator might also examine them for different layers of one architecture, but interoperability between their products is **not established** by the public material. [Vizgard homepage](https://vizgard.com/), [Scaleout homepage](https://www.scaleoutsystems.com/).

The prior qualitative quality assessments should not be summed into a vendor score. Vizgard's stronger mission-function clarity and Scaleout's stronger offer-boundary explanation help different decisions. The actual requirement, evidence and delivery scope would determine which distinction matters.

## Questions for a fair evaluation

1. **Buyer and requirement:** What is being bought first: an operational visual function, distributed model operations, onboard compute, or an integrated combination? Which organisation owns acceptance and operation?
2. **Configuration:** Which functions, models, sensors, hardware and interfaces are included in the baseline offer from each company? What needs project-specific engineering or a third party?
3. **Connectivity and data:** What exactly continues under each link-failure mode? Where do raw observations, labels, model updates and logs move, and who approves releases?
4. **Maturity and evidence:** For the proposed configuration, what has been deployed, piloted or only tested internally? Can each supplier provide comparable results under representative conditions and agreed acceptance criteria?
5. **Commercial and support:** What can be contracted now, on what licence and service terms, with whose integration, accreditation and lifecycle responsibilities?

Buyer interviews are needed to confirm that these are real comparison situations and to identify the actual alternatives. A technical evaluation on named equipment and shared criteria is needed to judge capability and performance. No overall winner follows from the websites alone.
