# Positioning quality assessment prompt

## Purpose

Use this after a [website positioning extraction and synthesis](POSITIONING_FIRST_PASS_PROMPT.md). The extraction describes the position the material communicates; this prompt evaluates how well that communicated position helps relevant buyers recognise, understand, compare and trust the offer. It produces a qualitative assessment, not a measurement of actual buyer perception or competitive performance.

## Reusable prompt

> Assess the **quality of the position communicated** in the supplied positioning extraction and its source material. Use the supplied buyer context if available. If no buyer context is supplied, infer plausible visitor roles from the source material, label them as inferences, and explain how this limits the assessment.
>
> **Input:** [Paste or link to the completed positioning analysis and, if available, the website URL or source text. Add known buyer roles, requirements and alternatives here.]
>
> Revisit source pages where needed to verify a judgement. Cite the source for factual statements. Distinguish what the site communicates from what its product can actually do, and distinguish both from how buyers actually perceive it. Do not treat a claim, logo, award, trial or deployment as proof of a different kind of outcome. Do not make up missing competitors, performance results or buyer feedback. Do not use a numeric overall score unless a calibrated rubric and buyer evidence are supplied.
>
> Assess these criteria:
>
> 1. **Relevant need:** Is there a clear situation or requirement that makes the offer worth considering?
> 2. **Target buyer:** Is it clear who should care most and who participates in the decision?
> 3. **Category and frame:** Can a buyer place the offer in a useful mental and procurement frame?
> 4. **Alternatives:** Does the communicated position reflect the options the buyer might actually consider, including the status quo?
> 5. **Offer and system role:** Is it clear what the supplier provides, what others provide, and where the offer fits?
> 6. **Distinctive buyer value:** Is there a credible causal link from capability to a valuable result, relative to alternatives?
> 7. **Reason to believe:** Is the evidence relevant to the claim, requirement and maturity being communicated?
>
> Use qualitative judgements such as **strong**, **moderate**, **developing** or **unclear**, with a specific reason for each. Separate weaknesses in the underlying strategic position from problems of explanation or missing public evidence. If an element cannot be judged from public material, say so.
>
> **Output in this order:**
>
> A. **Overall assessment:** A short verdict first, including what is strong and what is unresolved. State the evidence limit. Avoid a numerical overall score.
>
> B. **Assessment table:** Criterion, qualitative judgement, reasoning and direct source links. Keep rows concise and concrete.
>
> C. **Visitor perspectives:** Explain how the relevant mission sponsor or user, technical evaluator, OEM/integrator, and programme/procurement owner might interpret the position. Include only roles relevant to this offer. Label these as informed inferences unless supported by buyer research.
>
> D. **Evaluation and improvement priorities:** Identify the few positioning decisions or evidence gaps most likely to sharpen the buyer's understanding. Prioritise decisions about buyer situation, alternatives and value before copy edits. Do not recommend broadening claims beyond the offer or maturity supported by the sources.
>
> End by stating what would require buyer interviews, evaluation results or competitive research to validate. Keep this assessment separate from any rewrite of the website.
