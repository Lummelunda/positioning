# What we mean by positioning

**Working definition:** Positioning is the deliberate choice of how a specific buyer should understand and compare an offer: what kind of solution it is, which need it addresses, which alternatives it should be compared with, why it is valuable for that buyer, and what evidence makes that claim credible.

This is a synthesis of established frameworks, rather than a quotation. April Dunford identifies competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, customer value, target customers and market category as the components of positioning. Kellogg describes the target audience, frame of reference, point of difference and reason to believe.

We choose an *intended* position; the buyer forms the *actual* position. Positioning therefore has to be tested against how buyers understand the offer and its alternatives. Website copy, page structure, presentations and visual design communicate the position. They are not the position itself.

## From positioning to a statement

A positioning statement is a concise expression of positioning decisions already made. Its inputs are:

1. **Buyer and situation:** Who is the offer for, and when should they consider it?
2. **Frame of reference:** What kind of solution is it, and which alternatives will buyers compare it with?
3. **Offer and role:** What does it supply within the buyer's system?
4. **Distinctive value:** Why does its difference matter for the buyer's requirement?
5. **Reason to believe:** What evidence supports the claim?

These inputs need to be resolved before writing the statement. The statement need not force every detail into one sentence; supporting evidence can sit beside it. A polished sentence does not resolve uncertainty about the buyer, alternatives, offer or proof.

## Buyer-facing quality check for Scaleout

Can the relevant buyer:

1. Recognise when to consider Scaleout?
2. Understand what Scaleout supplies and its role in their system?
3. Compare it with the alternatives they would actually consider?
4. Understand the value of the difference for their requirement?
5. See credible evidence for the claims?

A useful positioning suggestion should say which of these questions it helps answer, for which buyer, and what Scaleout can truthfully support.
