# Vizgard: quality of the communicated position

**Assessed:** 27 September 2026  
**Basis:** [Vizgard website positioning analysis](VIZGARD_POSITIONING_ANALYSIS.md) and its linked public sources. This assesses the position communicated by the site. It does not measure buyer perception, product performance or actual competitive win rate.

## Overall assessment

**Vizgard communicates a recognisable operational position, led by FortifAI and concrete visual-security tasks.** A buyer can quickly see why automated visual analysis and camera control might matter. The position becomes less precise when several buyer groups, missions and products are considered together: the site does not make one priority buying situation clear, and the maturity and delivery boundary of each product need separate examination. A numeric score would imply more certainty than the public material supports.

## How different visitors may read it

- **Mission sponsor or operational user:** Can likely recognise tasks such as counter-drone surveillance, perimeter monitoring or UAV/USV automation and the intended reduction in operator workload.
- **Technical evaluator:** Can see named sensor types, integrations and edge-compute requirements, but would need configuration-specific performance, assurance and interface evidence before judging suitability.
- **OEM or integrator:** Can see a potential route to add visual autonomy to existing cameras and platforms; would need to establish supported configurations, control boundaries and the division of integration responsibilities.
- **Programme or procurement owner:** Can see named products, a FortifAI licensing outline and company-reported deployment evidence; would need to clarify what is available now in each product and what evidence supports acceptance in the intended use.

These are inferences from the public material, not findings from buyer testing.

## Evaluation and improvement priorities

The communicated position is **concrete and operationally legible**, particularly for FortifAI. The decisions most likely to sharpen it further are:

1. **Priority buying situation:** Identify which buyer and mission should lead the company-level story when the offer spans counter-drone, perimeter, maritime and uncrewed applications.
2. **Product scope and delivery boundary:** Clarify the standard FortifAI configuration, the roles of UnifAI and VizEdge in a deployment, and which functions require project-specific integration. UnifAI's “Join waiting list” action should be reconciled with its field-tested description. [UnifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/unifai/).
3. **Evidence of advantage:** Connect deployment and performance claims to specific tasks, test conditions and measured outcomes. This would help buyers interpret the claimed TRL, speed and cost advantages within their own requirements. [Homepage FAQ](https://vizgard.com/), [FortifAI](https://vizgard.com/product/fortifai/).
4. **Actual alternatives:** Confirm which options buyers compare Vizgard with in each priority situation, including in-house development and existing surveillance-system functions.

Buyer interviews would be needed to establish the actual comparison set and whether visitors interpret the position as intended. Product evaluation and customer evidence would be needed to validate capability and outcome claims. This assessment does not rewrite the website.
